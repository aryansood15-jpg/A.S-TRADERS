Veterinary Science & Herd Management

Science-Backed Nutrition Guide for High-Yielding Dairy Cattle

Understanding trace mineral bioavailability, rumen buffer dynamics, and post-calving calcium requirements is critical for commercial dairy success.

Chelated Minerals vs. Inorganic Salts

Traditional inorganic mineral mixtures often form insoluble complexes in the cattle digestive tract, resulting in over 70% mineral loss in dung. Amino acid chelated trace minerals (Zinc, Copper, Manganese, Cobalt) are bonded to organic peptide carriers, allowing unhindered passage through the rumen for 3x higher cellular absorption.

SARA Prevention & Rumen Buffering

Sub-Acute Rumen Acidosis (SARA) occurs when dairy cattle consume high starch/grain rations, causing rapid volatile fatty acid production and a sharp drop in rumen pH below 5.8. Incorporating Sodium Bicarbonate and Magnesium Oxide stabilizes rumen buffering capacity and maintains high milk fat percentages.

Lactation Calcium & Hypocalcemia Control

With the onset of calving, sudden colostrum production depletes blood calcium reserves, leading to clinical or subclinical milk fever. High-bioavailability liquid calcium fortified with Vitamin D3 and galactagogues ensures instant systemic calcium availability, promoting uterine health and smooth milk letdown.
